A student of economics keeps wandering into the mathematics department

What began as writing and research has turned, steadily, into a case for rigour — and a slow build toward the place where mathematics, economics and data meet.

By Ishaan Harish — New Delhi

I came to economics through writing and research — drawn to the way it explains decisions, institutions, and the structure of societies. It was, at first, a discipline of arguments: why people choose what they choose, and why systems hold or fail.

But the deeper I went, the more I found myself pulled toward the rigour of mathematics and the precision of quantitative thinking. The arguments were more convincing when they could be measured, and more honest when they could be wrong.

I am now building, slowly and deliberately, toward the intersection of mathematics, economics and data — through research, through code, and through a reading list that keeps growing faster than I can finish it.
